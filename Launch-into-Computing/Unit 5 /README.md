## 📝 Case Study for unit 05
Netflix, one of the world’s largest streaming platforms, utilises Data Science and advanced storage systems to deliver personalised customer experiences. With over 230 million subscribers globally, Netflix processes petabytes of structured and unstructured data daily to recommend content, optimise video delivery, and predict user preferences.

Netflix employs Data Science techniques, including machine learning algorithms, to analyse user behaviour, such as watch time, browsing history, and preferences. The company integrates these insights into a recommendation engine that accounts for over 80% of watched content, significantly improving customer satisfaction and retention.

To handle this vast data volume, Netflix relies on NoSQL databases (e.g., Cassandra) and cloud storage solutions like AWS S3. These technologies enable the platform to manage distributed storage, handle scalable workloads, and ensure low latency performance globally. Additionally, Netflix’s data strategy incorporates legal and ethical considerations like GDPR compliance, ensuring user data privacy and security.

Discussion Points
How does Netflix use Data Science and storage technologies to enhance its recommendation system?

Why are NoSQL databases more suited for Netflix’s needs than traditional SQL-based systems?

What challenges might Netflix face with data privacy and ethical concerns, given its reliance on user behaviour analytics?

Netflix’s success as a global streaming provider is closely linked to how effectively it applies Data Science and large‑scale storage technologies to personalise user experiences. With more than 230 million subscribers worldwide, the platform processes vast quantities of behavioural data daily, including viewing duration, search patterns, device usage, and interactions with recommended content (Netflix Technology Blog, 2023). Analysing this data enables Netflix to build detailed behavioural profiles that support continuous refinement of its recommendation system.

The recommendation engine is central to Netflix’s strategy. It uses machine learning models to identify patterns in user behaviour and compare them with similar viewing profiles, allowing the system to predict what content an individual is most likely to enjoy (Ghemawat, 2022). This approach reflects practical applications of supervised and unsupervised learning, where algorithms improve through feedback loops as new data is generated. Research from Netflix indicates that personalised recommendations account for the majority of content consumed on the platform, demonstrating how predictive analytics can reduce decision fatigue and enhance user satisfaction (Netflix Technology Blog, 2023).

To support this level of personalisation, Netflix relies on storage technologies capable of handling distributed, high‑volume workloads. Traditional relational databases are limited in scalability and flexibility, particularly when managing unstructured behavioural data. As a result, Netflix uses NoSQL databases such as Cassandra, which allow data to be stored across multiple nodes with high availability and low latency (Ghemawat, 2022). Cloud‑based storage solutions, including AWS S3, provide additional resilience and scalability, enabling Netflix to store and retrieve petabytes of data efficiently while maintaining global performance standards.

However, the extensive use of behavioural analytics introduces significant ethical and legal responsibilities. Regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) require organisations to justify data collection practices, ensure transparency, and protect user privacy (European Union, 2018). Ethical concerns also arise regarding user consent and the extent to which personal data is used to influence viewing behaviour. While personalisation enhances engagement, it raises questions about data minimisation and whether users fully understand how their information is being utilised. As Netflix continues to expand its data‑driven approach, maintaining trust through responsible data governance becomes essential.
